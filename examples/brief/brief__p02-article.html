Markets close at record high as Fed signals rate cut by July

SM
Sara Mehta May 23, 2026 · 6 min read

S&P 500 close, intraday — Reuters / Brief

The S&P 500 crossed 6,200 for the first time on Friday after Chair Powell told reporters the Federal Reserve has "growing confidence" that inflation is returning to its 2% target — language traders read as a green light for an interest-rate cut as early as July.

The benchmark index gained 1.4% to close at 6,213.08. The Nasdaq Composite rose 1.9%, while the Dow Jones Industrial Average added 0.8%. The yield on the 10-year Treasury fell to 4.12%, its lowest level in three months.

Powell's comments, delivered at the post-meeting press conference in Washington, marked the clearest signal yet from a Fed chair who has spent much of the past year pushing back against market expectations for faster easing. While he stopped short of pre-committing to any particular meeting, traders in the federal funds futures market now price in a 78% chance of a cut at the July 30 decision.

"The committee is now in a position to consider the timing of policy adjustments."

The rally was broad-based. All 11 sectors of the S&P 500 finished higher, led by real estate and consumer discretionary stocks — segments most sensitive to changes in borrowing costs. Small-caps also outperformed, with the Russell 2000 closing up 2.6%.

Not everyone is convinced the move is sustainable. Strategists at Goldman Sachs warned in a Friday note that "the path from here depends on whether the labor market continues to cool gradually, rather than rolling over," pointing to next month's payrolls report as the next critical data point.

For now, though, equity markets are taking Powell at his word. After a year of false starts and revised dot plots, the soft-landing narrative finally has a target on the calendar.
